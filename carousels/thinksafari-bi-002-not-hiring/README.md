# ThinkSafari · Beyond Intelligence 002 — "We may not be firing, but we're not hiring."

## Source (read in full on anthropic.com)
**Nathan Wilmers, Maxim Massenkoff, Peter McCrory — "Working with AI", Anthropic (The Anthropic Institute, technical report), 23 Nov 2026.** https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai
- Method: 66 interviews with people at 58 of Anthropic's US enterprise customers, all in the middle of adopting AI; qualitative; names pseudonymous.
- Verified facts used: "People at two-thirds of the companies we spoke to reported reduced hiring due to AI"; "only two had seen AI automation fully eliminate a position and lead to someone losing their job"; "10 companies reported hiring more due to AI, primarily into AI implementation roles"; "A third of these hiring declines were due to companies actively shrinking functions through attrition"; most AI uses add value by increasing output rather than cutting costs; the highest-value uses involved reorganising roles and workplaces.
- Quotes used: "We may not be firing, but we're not hiring." · "Two people plus Claude is the most amazing thing… if two people agree on something, just ship it." · "The closer you are to the money, the harder it is for models to excel in that job."
- Limitations (stated in the report): not a random or representative sample; value judgements are interviewees' own descriptions, not measured; the companies are Anthropic customers (and also use other AI tools); "None of this is a guarantee against AI-induced job loss."
- **Not used:** the "1% of gains went to worker downtime" and "55% output / 19% cost" figures circulating in newsletters. They don't appear in the report's text (they may come from its charts or PDF, which couldn't be verified here).
- Our interpretation is labelled on slides 6 ("Our reading") and 7 ("Our question, not the study's").
- Note the conflict of interest: an AI company studying its own customers. The caption says so.

## Caption
"We may not be firing, but we're not hiring."

That line comes from a new Anthropic study: 66 interviews at 58 US companies already using AI. Only 2 had seen AI fully replace a job. But two-thirds said they're hiring less, and a third of that came from teams quietly shrinking as people left and nobody replaced them.

So the job AI takes may not be yours. It may be the one that never gets posted, often the entry-level one someone else needed.

The study also found most AI value came from doing more, not cutting costs, and the work "closest to the money" was hardest for AI.

Honest caveats: 58 companies, all Anthropic customers, interviews not hard data, published by an AI company.

Who do you think notices the job that never existed?
