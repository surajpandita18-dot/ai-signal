# ThinkSafari No. 01 — The Social Media Sea Change

## Source
- **Anne Helen Petersen, "The Social Media Sea Change"**, *Culture Study* (Substack), 19 Jan 2025 — https://annehelen.substack.com/p/the-social-media-sea-change (read in full)
- Supporting data: Meta's opening slides in *FTC v. Meta*, April 2025 — share of time spent viewing friends' content fell from 22% to 17% on Facebook and from 11% to 7% on Instagram (2023 → 2025). Reported by Business Insider, Fast Company and others; slide reproduced at https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/meta-s-opening-statement-ftc-v-meta-platforms-inc/277951345
- Kate Lindsay's line "Boredom is when life happens", as quoted in Petersen's essay.

## Editorial interpretation
Petersen's argument isn't "quit social media". It's quieter and more honest: she didn't leave because she found discipline. The apps lost their usefulness: feeds went from friends to brands, so the cost finally outweighed the benefit. Meta's own court numbers back the "sea change". The payoff is about what returns when the scroll goes: boredom, privacy and experiences that count without an audience.

## Hooks considered
1. **We didn't quit social media. It quietly stopped being social.** ← selected (a contradiction, true to the source)
2. Only 7% of our Instagram time goes to posts from friends. That's Meta's own number.
3. The feed used to be your friends. Look who lives there now.

## Slides

2. Relatable — open the app for friends, leave having seen strangers and ads.
3. Deeper question — "Maybe the place changed." Neighbours on a village street, 1910 (archival) + Petersen's "neighborhood → glossy condo development of brands".
4. Discovery — 7% / 17% (Meta's figures), over 1910 Times Square billboards.
5. Story (type only) — Petersen deletes the apps; "I haven't come to value it less; instead, it's become less valuable."
6. Twist — not willpower; the place stopped giving back. Empty mall.
7. Bigger idea — "Boredom is when life happens." Commuter reading a book.
8. Ending — "'People' may not know, but I do." A lone skier, unphotographed by anyone she knows.

## Caption
We didn't quit social media. It quietly stopped being social.

Anne Helen Petersen deleted Instagram from her phone the day before Christmas Eve. She meant to put it back. She never did, and not because she found willpower. "I haven't come to value it less; instead, it's become less valuable."

Meta's own numbers, shown in court last year: only 7% of Instagram time and 17% of Facebook time now goes to posts from friends. The neighbourhood turned into a mall.

What comes back when the scroll goes is quieter: boredom, privacy, things that count even when nobody sees them.

When did you last do something nobody saw, and it counted anyway?
