01 Cover: THINKSAFARI · BEYOND INTELLIGENCE · 001 / "AI can think for you." / *But should it?* / The Thinking Machine
02 The shift: "For centuries, machines carried our physical weight. Now they're lifting the mental kind." / Write. Decide. Explain. Research. Create. / "Most of it saves real time. Some of it used to be how we thought."
03 The science: "We have always handed our thinking to tools." / Psychologists call it *cognitive offloading*: using the world outside your head to lighten the work inside it. / Writing: Socrates warned it would weaken memory. Printing: the abbot Trithemius argued for copying by hand. Calculators: arithmetic teachers objected. / "Generative AI is different in kind: it doesn't just store or calculate. It drafts the reasoning itself."
04 The question: "What happens when we stop struggling with hard problems?" / In 1983 the psychologist Lisanne Bainbridge named the "irony of automation": hand the routine to a machine, and people lose the practice they need when things go wrong.
05 The research: 319 knowledge workers shared 936 real examples of using generative AI at work. / How often critical thinking felt like less effort with AI: Knowledge 72%, Comprehension 79%, Application 69%, Analysis 72%, Synthesis 76%, Evaluation 55%. / The more people trusted the AI, the less critical thinking they reported. The more they trusted themselves, the more they did. / Source and limits line.
06 The twist: "AI doesn't remove thinking. *It moves it.*" / From gathering information → verifying it. From solving the problem → fitting the answer in. From doing the task → overseeing it.
07 Two ways: "Same tool. Two habits." / The substitute: Ask. Accept. Paste. / The thinking partner: Ask. Question. Rewrite. Decide for yourself.
08 The bigger idea: "In an age of instant answers, the rarest skill may be knowing *when to question one.*" (our interpretation)
09 Closing: "If a machine can do more of our thinking, *what should we keep thinking about ourselves?*"
