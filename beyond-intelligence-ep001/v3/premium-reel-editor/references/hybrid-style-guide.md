# Hybrid House Style Guide

## Creative north star
Premium documentary clarity + modern creator immediacy. The creator remains the centre of the story; cinematic inserts and motion graphics deepen the idea rather than compete with the speaker.

## Visual hierarchy
1. Human face and spoken idea.
2. Evidence or contextual B-roll.
3. Explanatory graphic.
4. Decorative treatment (use rarely).

## Shot language
- A-roll: stable medium close-up as the visual anchor.
- Punch-in: usually one deliberate alternate crop for emphasis or a new beat; avoid repeated micro-zooms.
- Cutaway: use when the narration introduces a concrete object, place, person, event, or mechanism.
- Full-screen insert: reserve for evidence, a powerful image, a quote with verified attribution, or a complex diagram.
- Split screen: use only for direct comparison.
- Screen recordings: crop to the relevant interface area; hide personal data and unrelated UI.

## Rhythm
- Hook: establish premise quickly; no ornamental intro.
- Context: give the viewer enough information to care.
- Escalation: alternate A-roll, evidence, and concise graphics.
- Payoff: let the key reveal breathe; do not cut away too quickly.
- Ending: close on a memorable thought, question, or implication. Avoid generic "follow for more" unless requested.

## Graphic system
- Choose one primary typeface and one optional supporting face available in the environment.
- Use 2–3 text weights maximum.
- Use one accent colour consistently; do not invent a loud multi-colour palette.
- Keep graphics aligned to a simple grid.
- Animate position, opacity, scale, or line drawing with restraint.
- Avoid default template styles, glowing outlines, excessive gradients, and cartoon icons unless the story calls for them.

## Colour
- Correct first, grade second.
- Natural skin tone is the reference.
- Keep contrast and saturation controlled.
- Match B-roll to A-roll without forcing a stylised look.
- If mixed lighting or phone footage limits consistency, prefer honest naturalism over aggressive correction.

## Evidence integrity
- For research stories, show the original source where useful: paper title, journal, institution, date, or verified article.
- Never imply an illustrative AI-generated image is documentary evidence.
- Add small "Illustration" or "Concept visual" labels when ambiguity is possible.
- Keep citations/source URLs in the asset log and, where relevant, on-screen source notes.

## Originality rule
Borrow principles, not a creator's identity. Do not duplicate logos, exact title cards, signature caption designs, proprietary animation packages, or recognisable sequences.
