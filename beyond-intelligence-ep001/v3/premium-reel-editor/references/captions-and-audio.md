# Captions and Audio Standards

## Hinglish transcription
- Retain the speaker's actual language choice and code-switching.
- Do not silently translate Hindi into English or English into Hindi.
- Keep common English technical terms in English where that matches natural speech.
- Choose Devanagari or Roman Hindi consistently based on user preference or supplied reference.
- Manually verify proper nouns, names, acronyms, numbers, and technical vocabulary.
- Use punctuation to show pauses and sentence structure, not to rewrite the speaker.

## Caption layout
- Phrase-based captions are the default.
- Aim for one or two short lines.
- Highlight a key word only when it carries emphasis or meaning.
- Maintain consistent baseline, margins, and line spacing.
- Avoid placing captions over the mouth, eyes, hands demonstrating something, or key evidence.
- Check readability on a phone-sized preview.
- Keep within conservative vertical safe margins; do not assume platform UI is identical across devices.

## Accessibility
- Maintain strong contrast.
- Do not rely on colour alone to convey meaning.
- Avoid flashing or rapid strobing.
- Keep captions long enough to read at normal speed.
- Provide SRT/VTT where feasible.

## Voice
- Dialogue should remain intelligible and natural.
- Avoid over-denoising and harsh noise gates.
- Use gentle EQ/compression only when needed and listen for artefacts.
- Avoid clipping; leave headroom.
- Use consistent dialogue level across cuts.

## Music and effects
- Music supports mood and pace; it must not dictate the story.
- Duck music under dialogue.
- Use transitions, risers, impacts, and whooshes sparingly.
- Avoid sound effects on every caption or cut.
- Log music/SFX source and license.
- Do not use copyrighted commercial music unless the user has rights or the platform's licensed music workflow applies.
