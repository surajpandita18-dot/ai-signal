CAPTION
A machine beat Napoleon at chess. There was a man inside it.

Stories you never heard, Ep 01: the people and ideas behind AI, told by an AI product manager.

In 1770, Wolfgang von Kempelen built “the Turk”, a chess-playing machine. Before every game he opened its doors to show the gears. It toured Europe and America, played Napoleon and Charles Babbage, and Benjamin Franklin saw it in Paris.

For 84 years, until it burned in a Philadelphia fire in 1854, a chess master hidden inside was doing the thinking.

In 2005, Amazon named a product after it: Mechanical Turk, where software sends small tasks to real people. Amazon called it “artificial artificial intelligence”.

In 2024, The Information reported that Amazon’s “Just Walk Out” stores needed a team in India to review about 700 of every 1,000 sales in 2022. Amazon said the team mainly helped train the AI.

Honest twist: the viral 2025 story that Builder.ai’s AI was “secretly 700 Indian engineers” was wrong. The Pragmatic Engineer, which had repeated it, retracted after talking to former staff.

My take as an AI PM: every AI has people inside. That isn’t the scandal. Hiding it is.

Where have you seen “AI” that was really people? 👇

TURK freebie (DM text): 5 questions before you trust an “AI-powered” product
1. Which steps are done by a model, and which by people?
2. When the model isn’t sure, who decides, and how fast?
3. What share of cases does a human check today? And what’s the plan for that number?
4. Where are those people, and how are they paid and treated?
5. If the humans stopped tomorrow, what would break?
